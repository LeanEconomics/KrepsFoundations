# Chapter 5 source map

David M. Kreps, *Microeconomic Foundations I*, Chapter 5, pp. 79–122. The
[examples and exercises](examples-and-problems.md) inventory gives the specific
laws, rankings, and numerical results. [Corrections](corrections.md) records
qualifications and transcription issues. The [frozen verification record](../verification/verification.json) confirms the full
build, fresh source elaboration, and all-declaration axiom audit.

| Source | Lean modules or entry points | Mathematical coverage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Section 5.1, pp. 79–85, equations (5.1)–(5.2), Axiom 5.1 | `ActSeparability`, `StateComparisons`, `StateExamples` | State-contingent acts, state-additive and subjective values, the sure-thing principle, common outside-event payoffs, state dependence, and the horse, advertising and weather examples. |
| Definition 5.2, pp. 85–87 | `SimpleLottery`, `LotterySupport`, `FiniteLotteryTables`, `CompoundExamples` | Finitely supported mass on an arbitrary prize space with nonnegative weights summing to one. Sure prizes, exact finite distributions, reduction of compound lotteries, support union for interior mixtures and both endpoint rules. |
| Proposition 5.3, p. 88 and pp. 95–96 | `proposition_5_3`, `affineInMix_eq_expectedUtility`, `proposition_5_3_unique`, `expectedUtility_affine_transform` | Primitive axioms iff expected utility of a prize index on all finite-support lotteries. Restriction to degenerate lotteries determines the index. Binary affinity extends by support induction, and alternative prize utilities differ by a positive affine transformation. |
| Mixture spaces, pp. 89–90 | `MixtureSpace`, `MixtureAlgebra` | Mathlib abstract convex spaces, including simplices and statewise products. `Mix` is binary convex combination at a closed-interval real probability. Symmetry, constant and endpoint laws, and reduction of two mixtures on the same segment are proved. |
| Proposition 5.4, pp. 90–95 | `proposition_5_4` in `MixtureRepresentation`, `AffineUniqueness` | Rationality, background independence and endpoint continuity are equivalent to a mixture-affine representation. Positive affine uniqueness includes universal indifference and unrestricted anchors. The reverse direction proves the two strict endpoint inequalities directly from continuity of affine functions of the probability. |
| Lemma 5.5, p. 91 | `Independence` | Equivalent some/all-background strict comparisons, equivalent weak comparisons, and background-independent indifference. |
| Lemmas 5.6–5.8, pp. 91–92 | `ProbabilityGrids`, `SegmentOrder` | Midpoint betweenness, strict monotonicity at every probability increment, indifference on constant-rank segments, and substitution of indifferent endpoints. Equal-step grids include the dyadic grids in the text. Endpoint continuity and density transfer the order to all real probabilities. |
| Lemma 5.9 and bounded-anchor construction, pp. 92–94 | `SegmentCalibration`, `BoundedCalibration` | An actual supremum cut gives an indifferent probability for every intermediate alternative. Its uniqueness under strict anchors gives a representing, affine calibration on the whole preference interval. |
| Unbounded anchors and uniqueness, pp. 94–95 and Problem 5.3, p. 118 | `UnboundedCalibration`, `problem_5_3`, `AffineUniqueness` | Three cases calibrate an alternative between, above, or below fixed anchors. Each finite set lies in a bounded preference interval, on which normalized local utility agrees with all global calibrations. No best or worst alternative is assumed. |
| Nonsimple lotteries, pp. 96–100 | `AtomicDensityLaws`, `AtomicDensityCounterexample`, `AtomicDensityMixtures`, `UnboundedPrizeUtility` | Actual Borel laws with a finite atomic part and a continuous compactly supported density. The counterexample's affine index distinguishes the density on [3,4] and the sure prize 2. An unbounded prize disutility yields an actual countably supported law with infinite expected loss. The Gaussian/exponential example has an integrability and expected-utility calculation. |
| Proposition 5.10, p. 99 | `MeasureMixtures`, `WeakApproximation`, `WeakUtilityContinuity`, `BorelExpectedUtility` | On Euclidean prize spaces and the nonnegative orthant, weakly continuous preferences obeying the three axioms have an integral representation by a bounded continuous prize index, and conversely. Finite-support laws approximate each Borel probability in Mathlib's weak topology. Continuity of the affine lottery index rules out unbounded prize values. |
| Proposition 5.11, pp. 100–101 | `AlternativeMixtureAxioms`, `AlternativeRepresentation` | Both directions of the equivalence with strict independence at interior weights and the Archimedean strict triple condition. The reverse implication proves endpoint continuity and excludes a change of rank upon substitution of indifferent endpoints. |
| Horse/roulette lotteries and Proposition 5.12, pp. 101–106 | `HorseLotteries`, `AnscombeAumann`, `CompoundExamples` | Finite state vectors of simple lotteries with statewise mixtures. The primitive axioms are equivalent to a sum of state-dependent expected utilities. Uniqueness gives a common positive scale and separate state intercepts, with total intercept their sum. The order of horse and roulette resolution is illustrated by actual acts. |
| Proposition 5.13, pp. 107–108 | `StateComparisons`, `SubjectiveLotteries`, `AnscombeAumann` | The primitive axioms and condition (f) characterize subjective expected utility for nonempty finite state spaces. Comparisons on constant acts identify common prize utility up to positive affine change. The constructed probabilities are strictly positive, normalized, and unique for nonconstant prize utility. |
| Section 5.4, pp. 108–110 | `StateExamples` | Finite-state ticket values and the inequalities/indifference implied by the World Cup comparison. The Harsanyi common-prior discussion is an interpretation of subjective probabilities, with no asserted equality of different individuals' priors. |
| Section 5.5, pp. 110–116 | `EmpiricalCritiques` | Common monetary laws in alternate frames, the Allais contradiction, Ellsberg's known odds and sure-thing contradiction, and the dependence on portfolio and payment timing. Claims about actual subjects, experimental incentives, and Knightian uncertainty are discussion. |
| Problems 5.1(a–e) and 5.2, pp. 116–118 | `NumericalExercises`, `ExerciseDomains`, `StateExamples` | Five literal numerical decisions, exact branch probabilities, certified rankings involving roots, and equivalence of state-dependent probability-weighted utility to additivity with state-specific utilities. |
| Problems 5.4 and 5.5, pp. 118–119 | `CompoundExamples`, `SubsetLotteries`, `ExerciseDomains` | Roulette-first lotteries over complete acts permit unrestricted prize utilities on acts. Whole-subset lotteries and marginal inclusion probabilities give different predictions. All finite subsets, binary-lottery/marginal correspondence and resulting additive representation are included. |
| Problem 5.6(a–f), pp. 119–122 | `EventSimpleActs`, `EventDecomposition`, `EventProbabilities`, `EventSubjectiveUtility` | Acts constant on finite partitions from an arbitrary event algebra, mixture axioms, refinement identities, adapted sure-thing result, eventwise utility, finite additivity, conditional null events, and the common-prize subjective representation. A signed-weight counterexample checks the qualification in part (e). |
| Problem 5.6 afterword, p. 122 | `CountablyAdditiveEvents` | With a sigma-algebra and continuity of calibrated values under increasing events, a countably additive probability agrees with every event weight. For larger acts the extension uses explicitly stated measurability, domination and almost-everywhere convergence. |

in Lean use this namespace. Module names in the table identify files. `Basic` and
`StrictPreference` are Chapter 1 prerequisite copies at commit
`f9c357ada1d9965dabd9eec694720ba585faf575`. The proofs of the lottery and act
representations start with these same weak preference, rationality, strict
preference, indifference and utility representation conventions.
